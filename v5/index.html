How it is built

One stack. Your company sits on top.

Team AI is designed as four layers, each with its own job. Governance is planned to run through all four, so actions are permissioned, logged and reversible where the connected system allows.

Target architecture, not a description of shipped features. Select a layer to see what it would hold for the weekly client review.

The workspace turns a running agent into work the whole team can see and control.

Permissions, audit and approval boundaries across every layer.

Ownership

Own the process. Rent the model.

Models improve and change every few months. How your company works should not change with them. Team AI keeps the company layers separate from the model layer, so knowledge, rules, owners and history persist when the model underneath is swapped.

Who it is for

First: teams that deliver for clients every week.

We are starting with B2B services teams that run recurring client delivery across several tools: agencies, consultancies and managed-service firms. Their weekly reviews, reports and account updates follow the same pattern every time. Today that work is assembled by hand.

This is our first focus, and we are still testing it. It is not a validated segment.

  • COO or head of delivery. They own margin and client experience.

  • Operations lead. They already maintain the spreadsheets, templates and automations.

  • Client work that ships on time and to standard. Fewer hours spent assembling it. Delivery that keeps going when one person is away.

  • Proposed trigger, not yet measured: a delivery lead is out or leaves, and a weekly client review stalls because the process lived in their threads.

How we charge

Pay for the work the workspace runs.

Customers buy a reliable shared layer: context, governance and run history that become more valuable every week. The bill grows with the number of processes running, not with the number of people checking them. Charging per reviewer would discourage the oversight the product depends on.
