Today

The tools do more, but your team is still the glue.

Your team’s AI work is scattered across tools, private chats and one-off automations. People copy context, pass along outputs and keep track of what’s happening.

Strong individual tools aren’t the problem — many already add team features. But individual power, on its own, doesn’t create:

  • Shared ownership — who is responsible for a process.
  • Reuse — the whole team using and improving what one person built.
  • Visibility — what’s running, what changed, what needs a decision.

Interchangeable layer — not the place where your knowledge lives.

What we mean
  • ✓Knowledge, processes and ways of working live in the company’s workspace — not scattered across individual accounts.
  • ✓They aren’t tied to any one model.
  • ✓They stay with the team as roles, bots and models change.
What we don’t mean
  • ✕Owning third-party model weights.
  • ✕A finished universal export-and-restore. That must be built and demonstrated before we promise it.

A change goes live only after it holds up on real work.

  • 01

    Per-bot choice

    Each bot can use a different supported model — fast where speed matters, deeper where judgment does.

  • 02

    Context stays put

    Knowledge, process steps and history belong to the team, so a switch doesn’t mean starting over.

  • 03

    Checked, not assumed

    Models behave differently. A switch is tested on real work and approved by the owner.

Not a promise of universal compatibility, and not a way to pool or transfer subscriptions.

Claim boundaries

A product vision — and what we’ll prove before we promise it.

Everything shown is a concept. These are the questions we answer with real teams before any external product promise.

01

Team access & permissions

Real roles, scopes, and who can approve what.

02

Export with proven restore

Context and automations leave and come back intact — demonstrated, not assumed.

03

Portability across models

Every switch backed by quality checks on real work.

04

Supported integrations

Which work tools, and which actions, confirmed one by one.

05

Decision & action log

A complete, trustworthy record of who did what, and who approved it.

06

Limits of autonomy

Where bots may act alone, and where they must stop and ask.

We’re not the only ones building here.Other products already offer shared agents, team context, workflows and human approvals. We don’t claim uniqueness; differences in self-hosting, data ownership and workflow portability are what we’ll need to show.
